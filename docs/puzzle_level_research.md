# Puzzle campaign research

Research date: 24 October 2026. This report separates **verified source statements**, **design inferences**, and **unverified details**. It informs the campaign; it does not establish human enjoyment or universal solvability. The engine analysis and validation reports are the authority for this project's rules and measured outcomes.

## Method and evidence limits

Internet searches and source retrieval were performed before final campaign generation. Eight games were compared, giving greatest weight to grid-based swap match-three. Twelve YouTube records were researched through indexed titles/descriptions and direct page retrieval attempts, plus one developer video reference. **Zero videos were visually watched or frame-analyzed; no transcripts were accessible.** Direct YouTube opens returned fetch errors/throttling or a page shell. No timestamps, exact board shapes, move budgets, or obstacle introduction levels are inferred from a title. This explicitly leaves detailed first-five/first-ten competitor observations incomplete. Older videos are historical examples, not authoritative current level data; [Playrix's gameplay guide](https://playrix.helpshift.com/hc/en/14-homescapes/section/150-gameplay-guide/?s=teams-1603891840) explicitly warns that evolving levels can make walkthroughs outdated.

Source-derived summaries below are intentionally brief. No competitor layouts, assets, proprietary data, or complete level sequences were copied.

### What these comparisons establishâ€”and do not establish

Verified recurring ingredients include explicit goals, limited actions, ordinary matches and stronger combinations, visually distinct obstacles, and advancement rewards. Exact commercial introduction schedules, move counts, completion rates, abandonment rates, and five-level recovery cycles are not established by these sources. The following are **original design hypotheses**, not claims about all eight games:

- Onboarding should isolate the main action, then practice it with another color and a modest geometry change.
- More goals create attention-switching demands; restricted spaces change which match setups are available.
- A board with many absent cells is not necessarily difficult; connectivity, run lengths, and refill access matter more than obstacle count alone.
- Milestones should feel like a combination of familiar skills. The next level can relieve pressure without removing variety.
- Clear completion feedback and preserved progress can provide achievement; forced replay, opaque randomness and apparent paywalls can undermine agency.

For all eight games, exact first-five/ten layouts, detailed obstacle introduction order, abandonment causes at particular levels, and recovery-level placement remain unverified unless explicitly stated above. These gaps are not replaced by invented observations.

## Academic evidence and enjoyment

All four user-supplied starting references were looked up. DOI/full-text retrieval failures were followed by primary repositories or indexed primary abstracts.

1. **Kristensen & Burelli (2024), Difficulty Modelling in Mobile Puzzle Games.** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1155/2024/5592373), [author-institution abstract](https://researcher.itu.dk/p/en/research-outputs/difficulty-modelling-in-mobile-puzzle-games-an-empirical-study-on). The abstract reports that combining cohort statistics and simulated data produced the best estimates across the studied scenarios, with neural networks most consistent among these models. DOI/Wiley full text was blocked; this summary uses the institutional abstract. **Implication:** a solver pass rate is a property of that solver and tested seeds. Human completion and attempt rates require human data; no human-rate conversion is justified here.

2. **Larche & Dixon (2020), The relationship between the skill-challenge balance, game expertise, flow and the urge to keep playing complex mobile games.** [Requested PMC source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8943660/), [PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33027060/). The abstract describes 60 Candy Crush players playing near-standing, easy and difficult games. Perceived balance was strongest near their standing; easy games produced less flow, while regular and hard games had comparable flow despite greater frustration in hard games. **Implication:** flow is not the same as wellbeing or enjoyment, and frustration should be measured separately. This experienced-player sample does not contradict gentle novice onboarding. PMC initially served a browser check; indexed primary text and the PubMed abstract were inspected.

3. **Kristensen, Valdivia & Burelli (2021), Statistical Modelling of Level Difficulty in Puzzle Games.** [Requested conference PDF](https://ieee-cog.org/2021/assets/papers/paper_205.pdf), [author preprint abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.03305). The paper models action-count distributions rather than only win probability and evaluates on Lily's Garden data. The conference PDF timed out; the author abstract was accessible. **Implication:** report successful move counts, spare moves, failures and deadlocks alongside completion rate. Do not label a found route as minimum moves without exhaustive search or an admissible proof.

4. **Lomas et al. (2017), Is Difficulty Overrated?** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3025453.3025638), [TU Delft author-institution abstract](https://research.tudelft.nl/en/publications/is-difficulty-overrated-the-effects-of-choice-novelty-and-suspens/). Three educational-game experiments cover over 20,000 sessions. Moderate difficulty motivated when chosen; easy games motivated most when difficulty was assigned. Moderate novelty outperformed extremes, and close-game suspense helped. ACM was blocked; the institutional abstract was read. **Implication:** do not assume hard means enjoyable. Alternate familiar practice and modest novelty, preserving choice. Educational-game findings are not a numeric retention formula for this match-three campaign.

Additional evidence: [The Candy Crush Sweet Tooth: How Near-misses Increase Frustration and the Urge to Continue Gameplay](https://d-nb.info/1107823471/34) was located as a primary paper. Its title/abstract-level search evidence connects near misses to frustration and continued play; a full statistical review was not performed, so no effect size is asserted. We explicitly reject rigging refills or manufacturing close losses to increase retries.

Player discussions provide qualitative warning signals, not representative surveys. A [Homescapes player discussion](https://www.reddit.com/r/HomescapesOfficial/comments/zm30i1) complains about repeated hard-level stalls, and a [Toon Blast discussion](https://www.reddit.com/r/ToonBlast/comments/1nh3rsv/is_the_game_getting_unfair/) raises perceived unfairness. These establish that some players voice such complaints; they do not establish incidence, causality, or actual algorithmic manipulation. Treat long stalls, unexplained rule changes and perceived lack of control as issues to ask about in human playtests.

## Resulting campaign design strategy

The following are design decisions and hypotheses for **Prismleaf Vale**, constrained by engine inspection, rather than copied competitor patterns:

1. **Levels 1â€“5:** simple readable boards, few color targets, generous moves, no mandatory flare/bomb production, no wall/frozen obstacle burden. Start with a single clear objective. Variation should retain readily available matches rather than relying on cramped geometry. Initial solver success must be checked with a weak policy as well as a strategic one; human observation remains necessary.
2. **Levels 6â€“10:** repeat familiar matching with gentle geometry/goal variation. Introduce at most one new supported concept at a time, with enough moves for exploration.
3. **Levels 11â€“30:** teach restrictions and special dependencies separately, then practice and combine them. Delay original wall-heavy/special-demand levels beyond onboarding. A special target must use this engine's actual creation and collection semantics.
4. **Levels 31â€“60:** mix target prioritization, meaningful connected shapes and manageable obstruction. Broad boards offer setup choices; constricted boards need explicit legal-move and refill checks. Avoid assuming bigger means harder.
5. **Levels 61â€“100:** increase efficiency demands and familiar mechanic interactions, while retaining periodic recovery levels and deterministic successful witnesses. Expert/master/extreme are provisional design labels until measured performance supports them.
6. **Recovery rhythm:** start each later five-level block with relief, build toward a milestone, but vary geometry and strategy. The five-level rhythm comes from the user's design brief; the literature above does not prove it optimal. Preserve a rising long-term baseline, not a strictly increasing per-level curve.
7. **Fairness:** no purchases, ads, external boosts or intentional bad refills required by the validation solution. No target whose only path depends on unsupported behavior. Random failures remain possible unless the runtime explicitly guarantees otherwise.
8. **Variety:** compare canonical geometry, not just JSON bytes. Include rectangles, clipped corners, broad crosses, stepped/diamond-like outlines, asymmetry and limited central obstruction where the actual engine allows them. Consecutive levels should differ in layout or strategic focus, not solely counts.

## Measurement and human follow-up

Use at least 100 seeded trials per level when practical, with documented agent policies and equal seeds across comparisons. Store winning action sequences and replay them in the shared rules implementation. Separate ordinary trials from stronger rescue searches. A seeded witness proves that trajectory, not every randomized board. Failures are evidence about policy and budget; a failed bounded search is not proof of impossibility.

Track completion rate, successful moves used/spare moves, initial/ongoing legal-action counts, deadlocks, reshuffles, special creation/activation and remaining goals on failure. Estimate uncertainty (for example, a 95% Wilson interval), especially near thresholds. A 100/100 sample still does not prove a 100% population rate. Distinguish structural complexity, measured agent challenge and human skill explicitly.

Human playtests should observe unprompted first moves, explanation of targets, accidental swaps, perceived fairness, attempts to win, enjoyment/frustration separately, and whether a player wants a break. Compare novice and experienced players. Ask whether a loss felt actionable rather than manipulating near misses. Any claimed retention, abandonment reduction or genuinely enjoyable extreme difficulty remains unverified until this testing occurs.

## Patterns to avoid

- Required specials in the first five levels; simultaneous introduction of several obstacles and goals.
- Copying a blast-game solver or assuming every advertised match-three game uses swaps.
- False solvability from JSON checks, one lucky run, or a simulator with different refill rules.
- Tiny move budgets as the sole difficulty knob; excessive frozen cells or walls that erase meaningful choices.
- Assuming every unsuccessful search proves an impossible level, or every greedy win proves a trivial human level.
- Treating competitor commercial retention features as evidence of player wellbeing.
- Claiming video observations, timestamps, human win rates, or exact commercial onboarding schedules that were not observed.
